This book was recommended by my therapist upon discussing self-esteem. It is a long, deep book and, since I enjoy dense books, her recommendation was on point.

## Actual self-improvement help

A lot of self-esteem-related books suggest many non-psychology-based approaches that can seem magical, or put another way, not for everybody. This book goes into the psychology of self-esteem instead of giving subjective advice.

## Many mundane examples to relate to

The writing style is basically a collection of examples and psychological principles behind how humans deal with self-esteem. I personally think the author did a great job putting a lot of examples on the table for me to assess or relate; that made the learning on my side a lot more assertive.

## Self-esteem is deep, it’s not just believing in yourself

From the title of the book, I assumed it was dense for no reason. What is it about self-esteem besides believing in yourself and not letting others take you down? Well, a lot. The author goes through six concepts that build our self-esteem (referenced as pillars in the book). All of them are different and deep on their own, so the length of the book is totally justified. As an example, one of the pillars is ‘Living Purposefully’. The idea behind this is to try to find something that moves you deeply, make it a part of your life and if you embrace it as a purpose, it will make you feel happy on your own. It can be teaching, charity, writing (as I’m doing now), joining a local club and so many other things. This pillar, I believe, usually manifests by involving other people and us helping them grow, since community is a big part of us as humans.

## Do I recommend it?

Well, it is a long book and is heavy on psychology as a science. If you can handle that, this book is great. If not, I recommend you look at a Jordan Peterson lecture about responsibility on YouTube and remember that repeating affirmations will not improve your self-esteem.
