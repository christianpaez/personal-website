This book has been recommended pretty much everywhere on social media and book stores and I have read other titles by the author, so my expectations for this one were high, sadly.

## The story seems poorly written

Without giving stuff away, the story is about characters taking life lessons from a Guru/Master of wellness. The main problem I had with the story was that it is very boring, not relatable at all, and it is very exaggerated at many times. I kinda get what the author was going for but the story was not engaging, and it was all over the place.

## Too many quotes …

This master of wellness mentions a famous quote from some exceptional person who made massive accomplishments for humanity pretty much every 2–3 pages(I may be exaggerating though). This gets repetitive really fast and the funniest thing is that I did not remember a single quote from the book upon finishing it.

## Could have been way shorter

I think this book is about 400–500 pages long, it is way too long for such simple (but not easy) advice the author is trying to teach. I think ‘The Monk Who Sold His Ferrari’ is less than 200 pages and its length seems perfect. I honestly think this book should have had a simpler story and probably 60% less filler quotes/content.

## Should you join the club?

Well, yes, you should. I do not think the book is good at all, but the message and lesson are great. I would rather recommend you read or listen to a summary of this book rather than the full title.
